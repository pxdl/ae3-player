    HOW THE CONSOLE PLAYS THIS

    H or ESC closes

    What this is

    A native re-implementation of the game's PS2 sound driver. It performs streaming ADPCM decode, live SPU2 envelopes, gaussian resampling, the driver's integer volume and pan math, and the hardware reverb network. Everything is driven by the game's own data (banks, sequences, and tables read from the game's IRX modules) — extracted from your own disc, in your browser — and renders on the SPU2's 48000 Hz clock.

    Authored volume (VOL NN AUTH, keys - = A)

    The sound driver boots with song volume 127, but the game never plays music that loud. Every music cue computes songvol = trunc(127 × volume_scale × options_slider × dolby), where volume_scale is a per-song value between 0.28 and 0.56 stored in the game's own BGM database. Sony mastered each song individually. At 127, dense songs overdrive the 16-bit mix bus; at authored levels only occasional transient peaks clip, and the real console clips those too. Use - and = to override, and A to return to the authored value.

    Exact vs tick clock (key T)

    The console's sequencer runs as a callback of the 60 Hz sound thread. Every MIDI event due within a tick fires in one burst, so event timing quantizes to a 16.7 ms grid. TICK reproduces that, and it reads as notes arriving late, like an emulator with slowdown. EXACT fires events at their exact sample positions and is the default. Use TICK when comparing against a recording of the real game, since the console bakes that timing into any capture.

    Slots (the 48-cell grid)

    The SPU2 has 48 hardware voices, shared by music and sound effects. The driver grants them round-robin and never steals a playing voice. If all 48 are busy, the note is simply dropped. Voices free up only at the 60 Hz tick, once their envelope has fallen below 2. This game's music peaks at 31 of 48 voices (33 on the tick clock), so it never actually drops a note.

    Looping (key L)

    CC99 values 20 and 30 in the MIDI mark the loop start and end. They are consumed by the game's sequence walker and never reach the sound driver. The console loops music forever. 64 songs carry exactly one marker pair, and the four Genie Dancer jingles play once by design. The export menu can bounce the loop body any number of times.

    Reverb (keys R , .)

    The SPU2's hardware reverb, preset STUDIO_C at depth 30, is set once at boot and never touched again by the game. Only tones flagged for reverb feed the wet bus, and they stay in the dry mix as well, since it works as a send. The network runs at 24 kHz behind the hardware's 39-tap half-band filter. The depth knob here is purely for A/B listening; the game never moves it.

    Gauss vs bright (key G)

    The SPU2 resamples every voice with a 4-tap gaussian kernel, which is why the console sounds darker than a modern resampler. It also lets notes struck below their root pitch alias audibly, and that grit is part of the real console sound. BRIGHT swaps in a catmull-rom kernel with cleaner treble, which is not what the hardware does. The authored export always uses gaussian.

    Bus saturation (red waveform columns)

    Voices sum on a saturating 16-bit bus, exactly like the chip, so dense passages can genuinely crunch on real hardware. At authored volumes it amounts to rare transient clipping. The CLIP counter and red waveform columns show every saturated sample.

    Odd but real

    Velocity is linear; the banks carry an identity velocity curve, so there is no squared taper. The pan table is not constant power, so a centred tone is about 2.5 dB louder than a hard-panned one. Both come straight from the driver's own math and are kept, not fixed.
